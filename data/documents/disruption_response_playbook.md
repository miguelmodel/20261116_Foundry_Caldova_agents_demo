# Caldova | Disruption Response Playbook

Fictitious demo data. Caldova, the suppliers, agreements and operational events in this document are invented for this demonstration. Effective date: 2026-11-03. Currency: EUR.

## Purpose and planning clock

Protect launch availability without treating every delay as an air-freight emergency. The fictional EU "Spring" launch is 2026-11-17; "Spring" is a campaign name, not a claim about the calendar season. Shipment status, stock and demand figures always come from the ERP system (via the Logistics Analyst), never from this playbook.

## Severity and response times

| Level | Trigger | Accountable lead | First response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| S1 Critical | Launch in 14 days or less and any flagship forecast to stock out | Head of Supply Chain | Convene within 2 hours |
| S2 High | Delay exceeds 3 days or several stores have less than 7 days of cover | Logistics Manager | Assess within 4 hours |
| S3 Monitor | Delay at most 3 days and all affected stores have sufficient cover | Regional Planner | Review next working day |

Use the highest applicable level. A +300% viral demand spike means four times baseline demand, not three times.

## Required response sequence

1. Verify the shipment, PO, SKU, cause and original versus revised DC arrival in ERP. Obtain carrier confirmation before booking.
2. Check stock and effective daily demand at every flagship, both DCs and donor stores. Distinguish DC arrival from store availability.
3. Compare wait, partial air freight, approved backup supply, stock reallocation and a simple combined plan. Show stockout days per flagship, incremental cash and assumptions.
4. Consult the expedite policy, supplier agreement and safety-stock guidelines. Do not automatically net a disputed supplier penalty against recovery spending.
5. Name the recommended option, required approver, quantities, dispatch deadline, residual risks and next update time. An agent recommendation is not a purchase or approval.
6. After human approval, Logistics books freight, Procurement issues any backup PO and Regional Planning releases transfers. Finance tracks claims separately.

## Roles and communications

- Logistics Manager: validates carrier slots, split-shipment feasibility and arrival estimates; maintains the incident record.
- Regional Planner: checks launch demand, allocates stock to flagships and identifies donor-store exposure.
- Procurement: verifies capacity, supplier quality approval, notification evidence and penalty eligibility.
- Head of Supply Chain: owns the S1 decision and spending approval within the stated financial band.
- Finance: confirms the cash basis and records a supplier claim only after evidence review.
- Retail Operations: notifies Madrid Gran Via, Barcelona Passeig de Gracia, Paris Rivoli and Milan Corso Vittorio Emanuele of the agreed allocation.

For S1, send an initial internal summary within 2 hours, a costed decision pack within 6 hours and updates every 12 hours until the risk is contained. For S2, provide a decision pack within 1 working day and updates daily. Flag unverified external news as context, never as the ERP source of truth.

## Decision record

Record the snapshot date, quantities diverted from the original PO, new purchases, reserve stock retained, gross recovery spend, possible supplier recovery and approver. Reassess whenever an ETA moves by more than one day or effective demand changes by more than 20%.
