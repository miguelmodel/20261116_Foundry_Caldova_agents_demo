# Caldova | Expedite & Air-Freight Approval Policy

Fictitious demo data. This policy and its authority levels are invented for the Caldova demonstration. Effective date: 2026-11-03. All amounts are EUR, excluding tax.

## Approval authority

| Incremental recovery commitment | Required approver |
| --- | --- |
| Below EUR 25,000 | Logistics Manager |
| EUR 25,000 through EUR 100,000 inclusive | Head of Supply Chain |
| Above EUR 100,000 | COO |

Approval applies to the combined incident plan, including new supplier purchases, additional road transfers and incremental air-freight charges. Do not split bookings to bypass a threshold. A supplier penalty does not reduce the approval amount.

## When air freight is allowed

- A verified S1 or S2 disruption will otherwise produce a flagship stockout or a material launch shortfall.
- Planning has evaluated waiting and inventory reallocation first, using effective demand rather than historical baseline alone.
- Cargo is quality-released, physically accessible at origin and not already aboard a sealed sea container. The carrier confirms customs and air capacity.
- Only the quantity justified for the recovery window is expedited. The standard demo quote permits up to 3,000 units per affected shipment. ERP compares capacity-sized quotes, not minimum booking quantities; Planning must right-size the final booking before approval.
- The incident owner records why the availability benefit justifies the incremental freight cost. Environmental impact must be acknowledged; no emissions estimate is implied by these fictitious rates.

## Cost comparison convention

The original PO, budgeted sea freight and original final-mile road delivery are already committed in every option. Waiting therefore has zero additional cash cost in the comparison.

Air freight diverts units from the existing shipment; it is not a second purchase. An eligible, fully credited unused sea booking costs EUR 1.20 per unit on the Chittagong lane or EUR 1.10 on the Ningbo lane. Incremental air spend equals diverted units multiplied by air rate less the credited sea rate. The original final-mile budget still covers those units.

A backup order is an additional purchase: count its full goods value plus supplier-to-DC road charge of EUR 0.65 per unit. Its unit-price premium is useful context, but is not the total approval amount. Its final mile also costs EUR 0.65 per unit because those are additional units. Reallocation incurs an additional EUR 0.65 per unit moved, with direct delivery to the receiving store.

## Worked example (generic, illustrative only)

For a hypothetical 2,000-unit diversion on the Chittagong lane: 2,000 x (9.80 - 1.20) = EUR 17,200 of incremental air spend. A hypothetical 1,000-unit Boreal order landed at stores costs 1,000 x (unit price + 0.65 + 0.65). Combine every component of the plan before checking the approval threshold: a plan whose components are each below a threshold can still cross it in total.

Actual quantities, stock positions, delays and costs for a live incident come from the ERP system and the Logistics Analyst, not from this policy. The original supplier purchase remains open: do not assume cancellation or a refund. Possible supplier late-delivery recovery is shown separately.

## Booking and control

Procurement checks purchase authority and available cash before issuing the backup PO. Logistics confirms that the diversion instruction reaches the origin handling team before loading. Split instructions are only possible before sea departure, so quotes assume an immediate decision.

Approval, booking, supplier acceptance and allocation release require a human. ERP tools and the Analyst are read-only and cannot execute them. If a carrier cannot fully credit the unused sea leg, obtain a revised quote before approval; the deterministic scenario does not price cancellation fees.
